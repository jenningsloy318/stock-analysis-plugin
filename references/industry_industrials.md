## Industrials & Manufacturing — Sector-Specific Metrics & Valuation

### Key Sector Characteristics
- **GICS Sector**: 20 (Industrials)
- **Primary valuation**: EV/EBITDA, P/E (cycle-adjusted), FCF yield, order backlog growth
- **Why different**: Highly cyclical; earnings peak ≠ fair value peak; backlog is forward indicator; capex cycles drive multi-year swings
- **Cycle sensitivity**: Early-cycle recovery (capital goods > services); late-cycle peak earnings (mean reversion trap)

### Industrial-Specific Financial Metrics

| Metric | Formula | Healthy Range | Red Flag |
|--------|---------|---------------|----------|
| **Book-to-Bill Ratio** | New Orders / Revenue | >1.0 (growing backlog) | <0.9 for 2+ quarters |
| **Backlog Growth** | YoY change in unfilled orders | >5% | Declining 3+ quarters |
| **Organic Revenue Growth** | Revenue growth excl. M&A and FX | >GDP+2% | Negative in non-recession |
| **Incremental Margin** | Δ Operating Income / Δ Revenue | 25-40% | <15% (no operating leverage) |
| **FCF Conversion** | FCF / Net Income | >90% | <70% (capex or WC absorption) |
| **Capex/Revenue** | Capital Expenditure / Revenue | 3-6% (asset-light) to 8-15% (heavy) | Rising without revenue growth |
| **Days Sales in Backlog** | Backlog / (Revenue / 365) | Sector-dependent | Declining while peers grow |
| **Inventory Turns** | COGS / Average Inventory | >5x (lean manufacturing) | Declining trend |
| **Aftermarket Revenue %** | Service + Parts + MRO / Total Revenue | 30-60% (mature) | <20% (low recurring base) |
| **ROIC** | NOPAT / Invested Capital | >15% | <WACC |

### Valuation Methodology

1. **Cycle-Adjusted P/E**: Use mid-cycle or normalized earnings, not peak/trough — Shiller-style approach for industrials
2. **EV/EBITDA**: Primary comp metric. Range: 10-14x for diversified industrials, 14-20x for high-quality compounders, 8-12x for cyclicals at mid-cycle
3. **FCF Yield**: FCF / EV. Healthy industrial: 5-8%. Premium if aftermarket >40% of revenue
4. **Sum-of-Parts**: Required for conglomerates (e.g., GE, Siemens, Honeywell) — value each segment at appropriate multiple
5. **Backlog-Adjusted Growth**: Forward revenue visibility via backlog/revenue days — discount companies with <6 months visibility

### Critical Industrial Analysis Factors

1. **Cycle positioning**: Where are we? Early (order recovery), mid (capacity constraints), late (peak margins, inventory build), downturn (destocking). Use ISM PMI, durable goods orders, capacity utilization.
2. **Aftermarket/recurring base**: Companies with >40% aftermarket revenue (e.g., installed base service) deserve premium multiples — predictable cash flows through cycles
3. **Pricing power**: Can they pass through raw material inflation? Track price vs cost spread over 3-5 years
4. **Backlog quality**: Not all backlog is equal — check cancellation rates, contract terms, customer deposit requirements
5. **M&A integration**: Industrials are serial acquirers — track ROIC vs WACC on deals from 2-3 years ago (lagged outcome measurement)
6. **Operating leverage**: High fixed-cost structures (factories) mean small revenue changes amplify earnings — model upside AND downside scenarios
7. **China/trade exposure**: Revenue from China + tariff-exposed supply chains — quantify tariff sensitivity per $100M revenue

### Industrial Kill Switches
- Book-to-bill <0.85 for 3+ consecutive quarters
- Organic revenue declining while peers grow
- Incremental margins <10% on revenue growth (no operating leverage)
- Capex rising >30% without commensurate order growth
- Inventory days rising >20% YoY (destocking risk)
- Backlog cancellation rate >10%
- ROIC below WACC for 2+ years (value destruction)
