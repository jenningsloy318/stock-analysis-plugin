# Consumer Discretionary & Staples Deep-Dive

## E-Commerce & Marketplaces

### Core Metrics
| Metric | Formula/Definition | Bullish | Bearish |
|--------|-------------------|---------|---------|
| **GMV Growth** | Total transaction value on platform YoY | >20% | <5% |
| **Take Rate** | Platform revenue / GMV | >12% (improving) | <8% (race to bottom) |
| **Order Frequency** | Orders per active buyer per year | >5x | <2x |
| **AOV (Avg Order Value)** | GMV / Total orders | Stable or rising | Declining (mix shift down) |
| **CAC/LTV** | Acquisition cost / Customer lifetime value | LTV/CAC >3x | <1.5x |
| **Fulfillment Cost/Order** | Total logistics cost / Orders shipped | Declining YoY | Rising (last-mile inflation) |
| **1P vs 3P Mix** | % of GMV from marketplace (3P) | 3P >60% (capital-light) | 1P heavy + margin pressure |
| **Contribution Margin/Order** | Revenue - COGS - Fulfillment - Returns per order | >$3/order and improving | Negative (growth at all costs) |

### Competitive Dynamics
- **Logistics moat**: Warehouse density drives delivery speed. 1-day/same-day capability creates switching costs.
- **Prime-type memberships**: Subscription programs (Amazon Prime, Walmart+) lock in spend and raise share-of-wallet.
- **Ad monetization**: Retail media networks (25-30% incremental margin) increasingly drive profitability. Amazon ads: $50B+ run-rate.
- **Cross-border**: Temu/Shein model (direct from China) pressures Western marketplaces on price; countered by delivery speed and trust.
- **Returns rate**: 15-30% in apparel e-commerce; must be modeled as direct COGS drag.
- **Unit economics**: Breakeven at ~$25 AOV for standard delivery; below that requires subscription offset.

### Key Players
Amazon, Shopify, MercadoLibre, Sea (Shopee), Coupang, PDD Holdings, eBay, Etsy

## Luxury & Premium Brands

### Core Metrics
| Metric | Formula/Definition | Bullish | Bearish |
|--------|-------------------|---------|---------|
| **Like-for-Like Growth** | Same-store revenue growth (constant currency) | >8% | Negative |
| **ASP Growth** | Avg selling price increase (price vs mix) | >5% (pricing power) | Flat or declining |
| **DTC Mix %** | Direct-to-consumer revenue / Total revenue | >70% (margin control) | <40% (wholesale dependent) |
| **China Exposure** | Greater China % of revenue | 25-35% (diversified) | >45% (concentration risk) |
| **Leather Goods Margin** | Segment operating margin | >35% | <25% |
| **Inventory Days** | Inventory / (COGS/365) | <120 days (scarcity maintained) | >180 days (overproduction) |

### Competitive Dynamics
- **Heritage and scarcity**: Brands with 50+ year heritage command structural premiums; new entrants cannot replicate.
- **Vertical integration**: Own manufacturing (Hermes ateliers, LVMH tanneries) = quality control + margin capture.
- **Pricing power**: Tested by never discounting. Luxury brands that discount destroy brand equity permanently.
- **Celebrity/cultural relevance**: Creative director changes can shift desirability (Gucci post-Tom Ford, Bottega under Daniel Lee).
- **Geographic diversification**: Reliance on Chinese consumers (travel retail + domestic) is the key swing factor.

### Valuation
- EV/EBITDA 15-25x is the norm; Hermes trades at 30x+ (scarcity premium).
- Price discipline = premium multiple. Any discounting signal compresses valuation rapidly.
- Sum-of-parts relevant for conglomerates (LVMH: Fashion/Leather + Wines/Spirits + Retail + Watches).

### Key Players
LVMH, Hermes, Richemont, Kering, Ferrari, Brunello Cucinelli, Prada, Moncler

## Quick-Service & Casual Dining Restaurants

### Core Metrics
| Metric | Formula/Definition | Bullish | Bearish |
|--------|-------------------|---------|---------|
| **Same-Store Sales** | Comp sales growth (traffic + ticket) | >5% (traffic-driven) | Negative traffic 2+ quarters |
| **Franchise %** | Franchised units / Total units | >90% (asset-light) | <50% (capital-intensive) |
| **4-Wall EBITDA** | Unit-level EBITDA (excl. corporate) | >25% of revenue | <15% |
| **Payback Period** | Initial investment / Annual unit EBITDA | <3 years | >5 years |
| **New Unit Growth** | Net new units / Prior year base | >5% | <0% (closures exceed opens) |
| **Digital Mix %** | Digital/delivery orders / Total orders | >35% | <15% (lagging peers) |
| **Labor Cost %** | Labor / Revenue | <28% | >33% (margin squeeze) |
| **Food Cost %** | COGS / Revenue | <30% | >35% |

### Competitive Dynamics
- **Franchise flywheel**: Royalties (4-6% of sales) + rent = high-margin recurring revenue with no capex burden.
- **Real estate strategy**: Owning land/buildings (McDonald's model) creates a secondary value stream and franchisee lock-in.
- **Menu innovation**: Limited-time offers drive traffic without permanent SKU complexity. Cadence matters.
- **Loyalty programs**: Digital loyalty (Starbucks Rewards, Chipotle) increases frequency and provides data.
- **Drive-thru/digital-only formats**: Lower labor, higher throughput; Chipotle Chipotlanes, Taco Bell Defy.

### Valuation
- Franchise-heavy: EV/EBITDA 20-28x (McDonald's, YUM) due to recurring royalty streams.
- Company-operated: EV/EBITDA 12-18x (Chipotle, Starbucks) with growth premium for unit expansion.
- Restaurant Brands International model: Acquire + franchise-convert + extract royalties.

### Key Players
McDonald's, Starbucks, Chipotle, Restaurant Brands International, Yum! Brands, Domino's, Wingstop

## Grocery & Food Retail

### Core Metrics
| Metric | Formula/Definition | Bullish | Bearish |
|--------|-------------------|---------|---------|
| **Same-Store Sales** | Comp revenue growth | >3% (real growth) | Negative real (below food CPI) |
| **Sales per Sq Ft** | Total revenue / Selling area | >$600/sqft | <$400/sqft |
| **Inventory Turnover** | COGS / Average inventory | >12x (fresh efficiency) | <8x |
| **Shrink Rate** | Inventory loss % (theft + spoilage) | <2% | >3% (margin destroyer) |
| **Private Label %** | Store-brand sales / Total sales | >25% (margin accretive) | <15% (missing opportunity) |
| **E-Commerce Penetration** | Online grocery / Total grocery sales | >10% and growing | <5% (underinvested) |
| **Gross Margin** | (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue | 28-32% | <25% |
| **EBITDA Margin** | EBITDA / Revenue | 4-6% | <2.5% |

### Competitive Dynamics
- **Scale procurement**: Buying power drives 50-100bp cost advantage vs smaller chains. Walmart/Costco set the floor.
- **Automation**: Self-checkout, micro-fulfillment centers, automated distribution reduce labor cost per unit.
- **Loyalty data**: Personalized pricing/offers (Kroger Precision Marketing) monetize first-party data.
- **Format diversification**: Convenience (small format), warehouse clubs, online delivery coexist; pure-play single-format is risky.
- **Private label**: 30-40% gross margin vs 20-25% on national brands; Costco Kirkland = $60B+ brand equivalent.

### Valuation
- Low-multiple sector: P/E 12-18x (minimal growth premium), valued on consistency and dividend yield.
- Costco exception: P/E 35-45x due to membership model, exceptional retention, and international growth runway.
- EV/EBITDA 8-12x typical; warehouse clubs trade at premium due to membership economics.

### Key Players
Walmart, Costco, Kroger, Ahold Delhaize, Tesco, Carrefour, Target (grocery share growing)

## Direct-to-Consumer (DTC) & Branded Consumer

### Core Metrics
| Metric | Formula/Definition | Bullish | Bearish |
|--------|-------------------|---------|---------|
| **CAC** | Total marketing spend / New customers acquired | <$40 (efficient) | >$100 (unsustainable) |
| **LTV** | Avg order value x Purchase frequency x Avg lifespan | LTV/CAC >3x | <1.5x |
| **Repeat Purchase Rate** | Returning customers / Total customers | >40% | <20% (acquisition treadmill) |
| **Gross Margin** | (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue | 65-80% | <55% |
| **Marketing % Revenue** | Total marketing / Revenue | <25% (brand-led) | >40% (performance dependent) |
| **NPS** | Net Promoter Score | >50 | <20 |
| **Subscription Retention** | % of subscribers retained after 12 months | >70% | <40% |

### Competitive Dynamics
- **Brand moat vs performance marketing dependency**: True DTC winners build organic demand (search, word-of-mouth); losers are arbitraging paid channels.
- **Wholesale diversification**: Pure DTC is capital-intensive (fulfillment, returns); wholesale partnerships (Nordstrom, Dick's) de-risk.
- **International expansion**: Unit economics often worse abroad (logistics, brand awareness gap, local competition).
- **Community building**: Lululemon ambassadors, Nike Run Club, Glossier community create unpaid acquisition channels.

### Risk Flags
- CAC inflation: iOS 14.5+ privacy changes destroyed ROAS for Meta/Google-dependent brands (2021-2023 DTC reset).
- Over-reliance on single channel: >50% of revenue from one platform (Meta ads, Amazon) = existential risk.
- Inventory build: DTC brands over-ordering to chase growth; watch days-inventory-outstanding spikes.
- Gross margin compression: Input cost inflation (cotton, freight) without pricing power = margin squeeze.

### Key Players
Nike (DTC pivot), Lululemon, On Holdings, Deckers (Hoka/UGG), Warby Parker, Allbirds (cautionary tale)

## Consumer Staples (HPC, Food & Bev, Tobacco)

### Core Metrics
| Metric | Formula/Definition | Bullish | Bearish |
|--------|-------------------|---------|---------|
| **Organic Revenue Growth** | Underlying growth excl. FX/M&A (volume + price/mix) | >4% | <1% (volume declines) |
| **Volume Growth** | Unit volume change YoY | Positive | Negative 3+ quarters |
| **Gross Margin** | (Revenue - COGS) / Revenue | >50% (HPC), >55% (Tobacco) | Declining 200bp+ YoY |
| **Market Share** | Nielsen/IRI tracked share in key categories | Gaining or stable | Losing >50bp to private label |
| **Innovation % Revenue** | Revenue from products launched <3 years ago | >10% | <5% (stale portfolio) |
| **Distribution Coverage** | % of relevant retail outlets carrying product | >80% in core markets | <60% (shelf space loss) |
| **Dividend Growth Streak** | Consecutive years of dividend increase | >10 years (Aristocrat) | Cut or frozen |
| **FCF Conversion** | FCF / Net Income | >90% | <70% (working capital issues) |

### Competitive Dynamics
- **Shelf space**: Physical retail placement is zero-sum; winning requires category captaincy, trade spend, and data.
- **Brand equity**: Willingness-to-pay premium over private label = brand strength. Test: price gap elasticity.
- **Pricing power**: Proven during 2022-2024 inflation cycle. Winners took 8-12% pricing with <2% volume loss.
- **Private label threat**: Aldi/Lidl expansion, Costco Kirkland, Amazon Basics pressure branded players in commoditized categories.
- **Emerging market penetration**: P&G/Unilever derive 30-40% from EM; sachet strategy, distribution depth matters.
- **Tobacco transition**: Combustibles declining 3-5%/year; smoke-free products (IQOS, ZYN) growing 20%+ = portfolio transition story.

### Valuation
- P/E 20-25x is the norm (defensive premium for predictability); 25-30x for top-tier compounders (P&G, L'Oreal).
- Dividend yield 2-4% with 5-8% annual dividend growth = 7-12% total return expectation.
- EV/EBITDA 14-18x; tobacco trades at discount (12-14x) due to secular decline and ESG exclusion.
- Valued on stability, not growth; re-ratings from portfolio simplification or EM acceleration.

### Key Players
P&G, Unilever, Nestle, Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, Philip Morris, L'Oreal, Colgate-Palmolive, Diageo

## Red Flags Across Consumer Sectors

- **Channel stuffing**: Revenue growth decoupling from sell-through (inventory building at retailers)
- **Promotional intensity rising**: Discounting to maintain volume = brand equity erosion
- **Market share loss masked by pricing**: Revenue grows via price but units decline = unsustainable
- **Working capital deterioration**: Days payable stretching while receivables grow = cash flow quality concern
- **Franchise system stress**: Franchisee closures, unit-level margin compression, public franchisee disputes
- **China/EM deceleration**: Luxury and staples over-indexed to Chinese consumer; policy/macro shifts amplified
- **Private label share gains accelerating**: Signals branded players losing pricing power or relevance
- **DTC brands adding wholesale aggressively**: Often signals failing unit economics in direct channel
- **Excessive SKU proliferation**: Revenue spread thinner, inventory complexity rises, shelf productivity falls
- **Management shifting from volume to "revenue management"**: Euphemism for price increases masking demand weakness
